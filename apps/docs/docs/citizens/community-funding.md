# Donating to a campaign

A **campaign** is a request for money, published by a verified
organization for a specific piece of work — a borehole, a culvert, relief
after a flood. You can give to one whether or not you have a CivicOS
account.

Before you give anything, it is worth being clear about what CivicOS is
in this arrangement. **CivicOS does not hold your money.** Donations
settle directly into the organization's own bank account. What CivicOS
provides is a public record: what was asked for, what came in, and what
the organization says it did with it. That record is real and it is
permanent. It is not the same thing as CivicOS guaranteeing the money is
well spent, and this page is explicit about where the line falls.

## Finding a campaign

Three routes, all of them public:

- **Community funding** in the main navigation lists every open campaign.
  Filter by category, emergency appeals, or campaigns run by a verified
  organization; sort by **Recently added**, **Ending soon**, **Most funded**,
  **Emergency first**, or **Near me**.

  Two of those are worth knowing about. **Ending soon** puts campaigns whose
  deadline is still ahead of them first — one whose date has already passed
  goes to the bottom rather than disappearing, because it may still be taking
  money. **Near me** appears only when you are signed in and have joined a
  community; it re-orders the whole list so the closest campaigns come first,
  and does not hide anything further away.

- **Discover** mixes campaigns in with issues, petitions and everything
  else near you, ranked by how close they are to your community.
- **Search** finds a campaign by name — useful when someone has sent you
  a link or told you a title.

Campaigns still in draft, awaiting review, rejected by the platform, or
**paused** while a concern is looked into do not appear anywhere — and
their pages are not reachable by direct link either. If a campaign you
had open stops loading, that is usually why.

## What a campaign page tells you

- **The goal, and how much has come in.** Shown even at zero.
- **How the money will be spent** — the milestones the organization
  committed to before it was allowed to publish.
- **Where the money went** — what the organization says it has spent so
  far, itemised.
- **Who is running it** — the organization, and whether it is verified.

## Giving

1. Open the campaign and pick an amount: **₦1,000**, **₦2,500**,
   **₦5,000**, **₦10,000**, or anything else you type in.
2. Enter your **email**. This is used to send your receipt. It is never
   shown publicly.
3. Optionally add your **name** and a **message**, or tick **Give
   anonymously**.
4. Before you confirm, the page shows you the split — exactly how much
   reaches the organization, and both fees deducted from it.
5. You finish on **Paystack's** secure checkout. CivicOS never sees your
   card details.

Only campaigns that are **published** or already **funded** accept
donations, and only organizations that have completed verification and
connected a payout account can receive them.

### The fees

Two amounts are deducted before money reaches the organization, and the
donate form shows you both in naira before you confirm:

- **CivicOS takes 2.5%** — the platform fee.
- **Paystack takes its processing fee** — currently 1.5% plus ₦100,
  with the ₦100 waived on gifts under ₦2,500.

Both come out of the organization's share. On a **₦2,500** gift, ₦62.50
goes to CivicOS, ₦137.50 to Paystack, and **₦2,300 reaches the
organization**. On **₦10,000** it is ₦250, ₦250 and **₦9,500**.

Amounts are shown to the kobo, so the figures on screen add up. If they
did not, you would be right not to trust them.

### Giving anonymously

Tick the box and your name is never shown on the campaign — not in the
donor list, not anywhere public. Your email is still recorded, because
the receipt has to go somewhere and because a payment nobody can trace
is a payment nobody can reconcile. Anonymous means anonymous _to other
citizens_, not unrecorded.

### Signing in first

You do not need an account. But if you are signed in when you give, the
donation is attributed to you, which is the only way to receive the
updates the organization posts to its donors afterwards. Give while
signed out and the record is still complete — you simply will not be
told what happened next.

## Your receipt

When your payment settles you are emailed a receipt showing the amount,
the campaign, the organization, and the fee split.

**It is not a tax receipt.** The organization received your gift, not
CivicOS, so any tax documentation would have to come from them.

## What happens afterwards

The organization publishes what it has spent, against the milestones it
set out. The campaign page shows three figures: what was received
through CivicOS, what has been reported as spent, and what has not yet
been accounted for.

**CivicOS cannot verify any of those spending figures.** The money went
straight to the organization's bank account; CivicOS never held it and
has no way to check a receipt or visit a borehole. Reported spend is a
claim by the organization, published under its own name, and the page
says so. What CivicOS does guarantee is that the claim is on the record,
dated, and cannot be quietly revised later.

For the same reason there is no "remaining balance" anywhere on the
page. CivicOS knows what came _in_ through the platform. It does not
know what is left in an account it cannot see.

### The final report

When the work is done the organization files a closing account: what it
achieved, with supporting documents.

If money was still unaccounted for at the moment the report was filed,
the page says so and names the amount — and that figure is **frozen at
filing time**. If the organization publishes more spending afterwards,
the live accounting above updates, but the verdict on the report does
not. A thin report cannot be made to look complete after the fact.

## If something looks wrong

Be honest with yourself about what CivicOS can undo here, because it is
less than you might assume.

**You can raise a concern about a campaign**, from the bottom of the
campaign page. Two groups can do this, and nobody else:

- **People who donated to it.** You paid for something; you are owed an
  account of it.
- **People who live in the LGA the campaign serves.** You can see the
  site. An admin in another state cannot.

That restriction is deliberate. A campaign was reviewed by CivicOS staff
before it published and is locked afterwards, so a concern is not an
appeal against the approval — it is a report about conduct since, from
someone in a position to know. Leaving it open to anyone in the country
would make it a convenient tool for a rival organization or a political
opponent, which is the opposite of the point.

You will be asked to describe what you have actually seen. A reason on
its own is not enough to send.

**Nothing happens to the campaign automatically.** A concern never pauses
a fundraiser, never hides anything, and is never counted by a machine
into an action. A member of CivicOS staff reads every one. If several
unconnected people report the same campaign, that is treated as a
stronger signal than one person reporting repeatedly — but it still
takes a person to act.

You can also report an individual update an organization has posted, if a
specific claim in it is false. The report control sits under each update.

**There is no refund mechanism.** Once a payment settles it is in the
organization's bank account. CivicOS never held it and has no power to
reverse it; a refund would have to come from the organization.

What CivicOS can do is **pause** a campaign. Only platform admins can do
this. It stops new donations immediately and takes the page down, so it
protects the _next_ donor — not the last one. Separately, every settled donation is
checked against the payment provider on a schedule, and any disagreement
— money credited to the wrong organization, an amount that does not
match — is raised for an admin to investigate.

This is the honest shape of the thing: CivicOS makes it much harder for
an organization to take money quietly and say nothing, and much easier
for you to check what they said. It does not make giving risk-free. Give
to organizations whose record you can read.
