The problem

Democracy is a five-year event.
Governance is a daily one.

Citizens vote, governments form, and engagement fades until the next cycle. In between, the questions that matter most have no reliable answer:

Has this petition received a response?
Did my representative address this issue?
Was that project ever completed?
What happened to the commitment made last March?

Without visibility, accountability becomes reactive rather than continuous — and public trust erodes on both sides of the relationship.

The product

Five roles. One shared system of record.

For citizens

Report community issues with photos and location. Sign and create petitions. Answer public consultations. Follow representatives. Get real-time updates when something they care about moves.

For representatives

A direct channel to constituents that isn't social media. Publish announcements, respond officially to issues, and build a visible public record of responsiveness.

For organizations & NGOs

Take formal ownership of issues, run consultations, publish projects and progress, and raise transparently funded community campaigns.

For government admins

See what a community is actually saying, at volume, with AI triage — instead of reading ten thousand submissions by hand.

For moderators

Content flags, audit logs, and platform-level review queues that keep public civic space usable.

The compounding asset

Every interaction adds to a structured, queryable public record of civic activity — the thing no petition site or portal accumulates, and the reason switching cost grows over time.

Differentiator · CivicAI

CivicAI is not a chatbot. It's a decision-support layer.

An institution receiving 8,000 consultation responses cannot read them. CivicAI turns unstructured civic input into structured, actionable output — wired into the workflows that already exist.

Classify

Suggests category, severity and tags as a citizen types an issue report.

Summarize

Themes, sentiment, top asks and recommended actions across a whole thread.

Draft

Announcements, donor updates and completion reports in plain, accessible language.

Narrate & assess

Explains metrics in words; flags campaigns a human reviewer should open first.

Why this survives contact with government

Every output is a suggestion, never an action. Nothing auto-publishes, auto-assigns, or auto-decides. Each generation is provenance-tagged with its model and timestamp, every risk signal must cite the evidence behind it and state the innocent explanation for the same fact, and the entire layer fails open — if the model is unavailable, the civic loop keeps running without it. That is the difference between AI a public institution can adopt and AI its legal team blocks.

Differentiator · community funding

Civic money, with a ledger citizens can audit.

Organizations raise for boreholes, clinics, flood relief and school repairs — against milestones, with spending disclosed in public.

Append-only by design

A settled donation is never edited. Corrections are new rows. The ledger is the only account of this money the platform will ever have — so it is built to be replay-safe and tamper-evident.

Funds never held by us

Payments settle directly to the organization's own payment-provider sub-account. CivicOS is the transparency layer, not the custodian — which materially changes the regulatory posture.

Milestones, updates, disclosure

Campaigns publish a plan, report progress, and file spending records against it. AI drafts the donor update; a human publishes it.

Stated honestly: organizations own their payment sub-account, so reported spending is disclosure, not something the platform can independently verify. The product is designed around that limit — visible plans, visible updates, visible gaps — rather than pretending otherwise.
